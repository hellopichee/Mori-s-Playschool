The Primary Planets in Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets, which are round objects that orbit around the sun. There are two types of planets: primary planets, and dwarf planets. In this lesson, we will discuss the primary planets in our solar system, which are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Let's look at each of these primary planets.

Mercury

Mercury is the planet closest to the Sun; this makes it very hot. Mercury is four times hotter than boiling water. In our solar system, there are four rocky planets, and Mercury is one of them. Mercury is also the smallest primary planet in our solar system.

Venus

Venus is the second planet from the sun as well as the closest planet to Earth! It is known as 'Earth's twin' because of its similar size and rocks to our planet. Because of how it is always cloudy on Venus, the sunlight never hits the surface of the planet.

Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and is where we all live. Earth is the only planet we know of that has oceans and life on it. Like Mercury, it is also a rocky planet. Earth has a moon that orbits around it, just like how the planets in our solar system orbit the sun.

Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun. Mars has a reddish hue, giving it its nickname 'the Red Planet'. But even though it is red, it is not hot. It is actually much colder than Earth because it is further from the Sun compared to our planet. Scientists believe there used to be water on Mars; if there was water, there may have been life at some point! Mars is the fourth rocky planet in our solar system. Like our planet, Mars also has a moon, but it actually has two very small moons.

Jupiter

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun, the biggest planet, and the first gas planet in our solar system. Jupiter is so big, it could fit all of the other planets inside and there would still be extra room. Because it is made entirely of gas and liquid, there is no solid surface, making its appearance change hourly. Jupiter has at least 63 moons!

Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and is the second gas planet in our solar system. It's easily spotted because of its rings that are actually made of ice! Like Jupiter, Saturn also has a lot of moons, which sum up to 62. Saturn is the farthest planet we can see without a telescope.

Uranus

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and is the third gas planet. Uranus alongside Neptune are the only planets we cannot see without a telescope, which is why it took a longer time to discover these planets. Just like Saturn, Uranus also has rings that are believed to be parts of broken moons — speaking of moons, Uranus has 27 moons!

Neptune

Neptune is the eight and final planet and is the last of the four gas planets. Neptune was discovered while Uranus was being studied in 1846. Neptune is known for its storms — the worst storms in the solar system! Winds reach over 1,000 miles per hour on Neptune. Neptune has 14 known moons, but scientists believe there may be more.
